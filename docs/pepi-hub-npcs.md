# Pepi and the two trench-coat visitors

The implementation adds two elderly baboon visitors in trench coats to the main Hub, Pepi's free twelve-choice catalog, a voluntary first-person injection gag and twelve temporary game effects. The visitors have procedural narrow bodies, long baboon muzzles, grey brows and whiskers, bent knees and tails. They reuse the Hub terrain/collision queries, path lanes, action button, modal HUD, pilot camera handoff, environment-sound bus and pile-return navigation. Both baboons amble on all fours and alternate twenty original comic doomsday lines in the same speech bubbles as Oogas. A brief interaction lifts the addressed baboon upright; Pepi stays upright through his offer and catalog, then returns smoothly to all fours. Reduced motion changes posture immediately.

This is a local gameplay implementation. NPC roaming, coat presentation, foreground arms, echo, cloud and knockout stars are not synchronized between visitors. The controlled Ooga's ordinary movement continues through the existing pose broadcast. There is no new room-state protocol, shared combat, persistent syringe inventory, reward, loot, payment or score change. The OBL codes are fictional game identifiers with the effects stated below.

The close shot uses new procedural foreground arms colored from the driven Ooga's skin. The ordinary Ooga rig has no elbow articulation, so the cinematic does not bend or inject the actual world-space arm mesh. Big Oops is a brief theatrical knockout and safe return, rather than actual character death. Earlier focused checks passed, but the broader follow-up QA is not green; its remaining failures and inspection limits are recorded below and in the full QA report.

## Player flow

1. Both visitors meander between suitable spots in the main Hub meadow. Their movement follows the existing Hub path graph with full-body swept terrain/prop clearance, supported ground, fire avoidance and separation from each other. A bounded local search detours around obstacles; lack of forward progress and trip deadlines trigger recovery or a new destination, so sliding along a wall cannot reset the watchdog forever. They hobble at 0.8 m/s, half the crew's standard 1.6 m/s NPC walk (ordinary randomized strolls range from 1.3 to 1.8 m/s). They do not enter caves or join the contributor crew. Their positions are local to each browser. Their stable body records also join the crew's existing outside-actor shoulder/gap avoidance, so crew walkers recognize them; they sweep against current remote bodies as well.
2. A signed-in visitor driving their own permitted Ooga comes within **2.75 metres, about nine feet**, of Pepi. This replaces the initial five-foot distance after play-test feedback about crowding. The range is measured between the actors' foot positions, including vertical separation. On stable ground, Pepi stops, rises from all fours and asks **“Looking for some pep?”** in the same wood-panel native modal style as the game's recipe and donation dialogs, with **Yes** and **No**. Only the local player's controls pause; the world and other Oogas continue. The coat stays closed. Its question uses the shared stone help panel, standard action buttons and close control; no custom cream speech bubble, tail or anchored position remains. Another open dialog, typing, a jump, a jetpack or competing interaction defers the offer.
3. **No**, Escape, outside dismissal or the close button returns to the previous camera and controls without a sample, effect or cooldown charge. Automatic offers stay suppressed until separation exceeds 3.35 m, with an eight-second decline cooldown. **Yes** opens the coat and frames its stock, without selecting or consuming anything. Pepi's left arm rests behind the enlarged leaf and all twelve syringe silhouettes are separated. The catalog sits beside the stock on desktop or below it on a phone; cards grow to fit their complete text, and the twelve choices scroll. Names and codes distinguish the choices without relying on color. Each entry states the effect, duration, free price and caution. The chosen code determines the outcome; there is no hidden roll.
4. Claiming takes one sample and starts a **60-second claim cooldown**. The action button becomes **Inject Yourself**. One sample can be held and one effect can be active; effects cannot stack. The held sample may be discarded or kept for later within this Hub visit.
5. Inject Yourself opens a confirmation with the chosen outcome and its downside. Confirming starts only when the visitor owns the Ooga and it is available and stationary on the ground. Jetpacks, seats, ladders, sleep and competing interactions must be finished first.
6. The pilot hands control to the close shot. The camera moves to the Ooga's eye, the palm-up left forearm rises, and the right hand brings the labeled syringe to it and depresses its plunger. The shot is non-graphic. A synthesized gorilla yell and an OOGA caption follow. Environment mute also mutes the yell.
7. Only completion consumes the held sample and applies its effect. Escape or the action button cancels the cinematic, restores camera/control ownership and retains the sample if its owner is still valid. A changed owner, logout, forced movement or stun cancels without replaying a stale actor position.
8. The status strip names the active effect and its remaining seconds. Expiry restores ordinary movement and removes temporary visuals and sound. Leaving the Hub or losing the valid owner also clears the held sample and effect. The claim cooldown remains page-local on `world.pepiClaim`, so a Hub round trip does not reset it; it is not a server-enforced or cross-tab limit.

Both baboons mutter while on all fours. A single scheduler alternates speakers every eleven seconds while in earshot (24 m from the camera), with one 5.5-second bubble at a time. Each speaker has ten lines. The stabilized Ooga speech renderer handles bubble visibility and scenery occlusion. Quiet synthesized, pitch-distinct babble accompanies the captions for about a second; this is gibberish rather than spoken-word TTS, uses the existing environment mute and attenuates with distance. Audio requires browser user activation. It is ambient and may be heard from offscreen within earshot.

All muttering pauses for Pepi dialogs, any other native dialog, typing, cinematics and competing interactions. Hiding, suspension and disposal hush both bubbles; suspension/disposal also close the mutter audio context. The lines and audio remain local and never enter multiplayer chat or microphone channels. Tapping the navy companion produces a three-second stand-and-look response; a new line is allowed only during a shared speech gap and with a twelve-second reply cooldown. It adds no shop, new name or gameplay role.

## The twelve recommended and implemented choices

| Code | Name | Duration | What it does | Tradeoff or limit |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| OBL-01 | Moon Knees | 30 s | Increases jump launch speed by 30%. | Ceilings and cliff collision remain active; this is launch speed, not a promise of a particular apex height. |
| OBL-02 | Rocket Socks | 30 s | Increases ordinary walking and running speed by 40%. | Edges arrive sooner. There is no added braking penalty. |
| OBL-03 | Feather Fall | 30 s | Halves gravity while the effect is active. | Jumps stay airborne longer and falls slow down; the abyss still returns the Ooga home. |
| OBL-04 | Triple Hop | 30 s | Allows three ordinary jump impulses before landing. | Requires another jump press in the air; ceilings still block motion. For an Ooga with built-in foot rockets, these ordinary jumps replace its ground-only powered hop while active. |
| OBL-05 | Crab Walk | 30 s | Makes pure strafing 60% faster and forward/backward movement 35% slower; diagonal input blends those factors. | Useful sideways, awkward when moving forward. |
| OBL-06 | Echo Buddy | 30 s | Places a harmless visual echo of the Ooga close behind it. | Visible only in the local view. It has no collision, target, attack, collection or distraction logic. |
| OBL-07 | Jelly Legs | 20 s | Reduces jump launch speed to 55%. | A deliberate impairment; use stairs and paths instead of gaps. Walking speed stays ordinary. |
| OBL-08 | Squeaky Sneakers | 30 s | Increases walking/running speed by 20% and adds pooled synthesized squeaks to grounded footsteps. | Squeaks follow environment mute and browser audio availability. They do not enter voice chat or alert AI. |
| OBL-09 | Personal Raincloud | 20 s | Adds a small following cloud and a fixed pool of falling raindrops above the Ooga. | A local cosmetic nuisance, without weather, visibility or traction changes. Other players do not receive this marker. |
| OBL-10 | Magnet Mittens | 20 s | Reduces ordinary walking and running speed to 60%. | A deliberate impairment; it does not attract items or change jump launch speed. |
| OBL-11 | Crash Out | 15 s | Walks toward nearby Oogas in the same zone, stops at a clear distance and shouts comic profanities in local speech bubbles with a synthesized grunt. | Temporarily owns walking. Steering, Escape, Space, controller action or the STOP button ends it. Targets beyond 10 m, in other zones, blocked, missing or unreachable are skipped. It never attacks or sends chat or microphone audio to other players. |
| OBL-12 | Big Oops | 3 s | Briefly locks controls, tilts the rendered pose and orbits stars around the head, then calls the Hub's existing return-to-pile navigation. | Explicitly warned before confirmation. No health, ammunition, equipment, inventory or score is lost; there is no death or gear-drop event. |

Three original suggestions needed replacements to fit the current signed-in Hub systems honestly. **Bubblehide → Feather Fall** avoids claiming a shared one-hit shield where no appropriate signed-in combat contract exists. **Pocket Tornado → Triple Hop** avoids changing pickup ownership or the economy. **Gecko Toes → Crab Walk** avoids advertising general wall climbing that the Ooga controller does not support. Rocket Socks keeps its speed benefit without an invented braking mechanic. Echo Buddy and Personal Raincloud remain explicit local cosmetics. Big Oops is selected directly, not a rare surprise hidden in another sample.

Movement modifiers affect the locally driven owner through ordinary collision and support checks. They do not change another Ooga, weapon damage, cabinet rules, leaderboard records or room authority. Temporary modifiers are cleared synchronously when crew control is released or disposed. Effect ownership is also checked each frame; an unavailable owner, stun, grab, sleep/bed transition, seat, rolling/burning state, ladder or jetpack ends the effect. Crash Out uses ordinary crew walking with sampled ground support, collision, fire avoidance and a fixed target cap. It does not intentionally walk off ledges, teleport, fire or damage anyone. The Ooga's movement uses the existing ordinary pose broadcast; profane bubbles and grunt audio are local only. Shared profanity delivery or voiced words would need a separate multiplayer/audio design.

The catalog is headed **Pepi's Peptides** and borrows the camera only after Yes. It hides the local player's foreground body while browsing, reserves clear screen space for the physical stock and tries collision-checked viewpoints. A blocked camera travel path uses a cut instead of moving through scenery; reduced motion also cuts. Close, claim, logout, body changes and scene departure restore the previous view without replaying a stale actor position. Camera holding uses the existing pilot capture/restore path, and autonomous Crash Out releases a held replay before walking. Its real action-button/gamepad stop path runs before menu gating, so the player can regain control.

## Cinematic and lifecycle details

The foreground rig makes the left palm, thumb and finger creases readable, articulates a right forearm and hand, and shares the catalog's selected syringe model, label, liquid and plunger. The ordinary head and arms are hidden only from this camera during the close shot. The two local NPC rigs also leave that camera's view during the shot so Pepi's open coat cannot obstruct the foreground hands at close range. Their prior visibility, camera near plane, pilot pose and control state are restored when it finishes or cancels. The real body remains on its supported position throughout.

Reduced-motion preference selects a shorter sequence with an immediate eye transition instead of the normal camera pan. The visible forearm/hand action and caption still establish what was chosen. There is no screen shake. The yell and the optional foot squeak each use reusable synthesized audio, routed through the existing environment mute and closed during cleanup; audio capability is not required to complete the interaction.

All temporary nodes are bounded: two NPCs, twelve displayed syringes, one foreground syringe, six echo body parts, twelve rain streaks and three knockout stars. Their geometry participates in Hub liveness. Hub suspension clears sample/effect/cinematic state and sound, hides the visitor group and retains the cooldown; resume restores the local visitors. Full disposal removes their nodes and targets through the existing Hub leave path.

Implementation files: [`pepi.js`](../src/js/pepi.js) owns interaction and dialogue state, [`pepi-roaming.js`](../src/js/pepi-roaming.js) routes and obstacle recovery, [`pepi-models.js`](../src/js/pepi-models.js) the baboons, coats and syringes, [`pepi-cinematic.js`](../src/js/pepi-cinematic.js) the camera shot and roar, and [`pepi-effects.js`](../src/js/pepi-effects.js) the catalog and effect lifecycle. [`crew.js`](../src/js/crew.js) provides the temporary local movement slot; [`scene-hub.js`](../src/js/scene-hub.js), [`hud.js`](../src/js/hud.js), markup and styles integrate the feature.

## Further UX improvements to consider

- Highlight the matching physical syringe when its catalog card receives pointer or keyboard focus, making the coat and list feel like one interaction.
- Add compact text badges such as “Boost”, “Downside”, “Cosmetic” and “Takes control” so players can scan the twelve outcomes before reading the details.
- Give Pepi a readable name badge or distinctive idle gesture so players can find him without the local preview's Find Pepi button.
- Add explicit controller focus navigation for Yes/No and the stock list. The current pad action can stop Crash Out, but full dialog navigation has not been implemented or tested on physical hardware.
- After one complete viewing, offer an optional shortened injection sequence while keeping the selected effect, confirmation and consumption rules unchanged.

These are recommendations, separate from the implemented spacing, offer, stock layout, control restoration and Crash Out changes.
